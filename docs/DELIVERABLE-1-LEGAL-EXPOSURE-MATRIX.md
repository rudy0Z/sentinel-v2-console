# Deliverable 1 — Legal & Regulatory Exposure Matrix

**Sentinel Command Console & TALON Copilot · 2026-11-04**
**Posture:** Senior Director of Product Design · Human Factors · Public Safety Technology Legal Advisor
**Object:** the live codebase, not the portfolio story about it.

Each row: exposure domain · what the product currently does (file) · real-world requirement or failure state · who is sued, cited, or indicted · severity · what a defensible design would do.

Severity is residual risk **if this build were placed in front of a live incident**, not the risk inside the exercise frame.

## 0 · Ground rules

The console is a two-tier authority prototype: `standing` (must cite SO/run card) and `operator` (effects only in `Talon.answer()`). Typed chat cannot authorize a gate. Silence is never approval. The evidence export is a SHA-256 integrity digest the product itself refuses to call a signature (`commandConsole.ts:1067,1098`). DEEP-AUDIT findings are F1–F13. F1 (note-kind interrupts have no card) is **not** closed. Dir. E. Lin’s “statutory issuer” role is a roster string. ISA-18.2 is a 2-per-10-scene-minutes cap, not an ISA-18.2 HMI program.

## 2 · Chain of custody & evidentiary discovery

| Domain | What the product does | Real-world requirement / failure state | Liability vector | Sev. | Defensible design |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| The export | `exportPackage()` (`commandConsole.ts:1069–1114`) writes JSON+MD with incident id, hardcoded operator, “Operational period 1,” refs, measures, decisions, dissents, standing count, demoted count, comms **counts** (not the log), and a SHA-256 of the JSON. Copy is exactly right: “not a signature and not legal attestation.” | A grand-jury / OSHA / NTSB-style inquiry wants: who was on the console, who else was in the room, what the AI recommended and on what basis, what the human saw at the moment of decision, what was sent to whom over what net, wall-clock + scene-time, and a chain of custody a third party can re-verify. FRE 902(13)/(14) self-authentication typically wants a hash **plus** a custodian, a system description, and a process in place **before** the incident. A post-hoc JSON dump is a **copy**, not a record. | Everyone. Plaintiffs will call the missing comms log and the missing second operator a spoliation story. | **Critical** | Keep the honest copy. Add before any live use: (1) authenticated operator identity; (2) append-only event log including `message_unit`, model replies, demotions, acks, and open-card/map-view; (3) wall clock **and** scene time on every entry; (4) named custodian and retention class (CPRA/FOIA); (5) hash chain or RFC 3161 timestamp. Do **not** market this as “court-ready.” |
| AI recommendations as trial exhibits | Every TALON card is recommended-first with `why[]`, `basis`, confidence, provenance. Dissent keeps both positions verbatim. | “TALON recommended X and the operator did Y” will be Exhibit A in both directions. The over-broad dissent trigger (F4) writes a “dissent” record for **routine tactical alternates**, which reads in discovery like a history of the operator overruling safety advice. | Operator, personally, in a wrongful-death case; agency in a negligent-entrustment / design-defect case against the vendor. | **Critical** | Ship F4: dissent ritual only for gated / life-safety conflict; otherwise “Alternate taken.” Never let “dissent” mean “picked the other COA.” Ban operator-reliability scoring from dissent history. |
| Overrides, standing actions, demotions | Standing actions logged with citation. Budget demotions logged. Lapses logged with “Nothing was sent.” | Discovery will ask what the system did without the human, and what it wanted to do and was stopped from doing. The unguarded `message_unit` path is the hole. | Vendor + agency if a crew acted on an uncited model transmission. | **High** | Enforce SO-4 in `execTool`: unit assigned, text cites the standing order, transmission logged as TALON’s. ## 3 · Aviation & autonomous drone jurisprudence

The codebase contains **zero** occurrences of Part 107, BVLOS, Remote ID, RPIC, LAANC, see-and-avoid, Part 89, or public-aircraft COA. The only aviation legal object is a TFR string.

| Domain | What the product does | Real-world requirement / failure state | Liability vector | Sev. | Defensible design |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| BVLOS / RPIC | Docks launch autonomously on standing orders. No RPIC is named. No visual observer. No DAA. No waiver number. | 14 CFR § 107.31 (visual line of sight) requires a waiver for BVLOS. As of this audit, FAA Part 108 is **proposed and not in effect**; BVLOS still requires a Part 107 waiver, a § 44807 exemption, a Part 135 certificate, or a **public-aircraft COA**. A Part 107 certificate alone does not permit BVLOS. The RPIC is legally responsible for the aircraft. | RPIC (if one exists and is unnamed in the record — worse), operator, agency, vendor that sold “autonomous” as if it were a waiver. FAA enforcement; NTSB; criminal exposure after a mid-air. | **Critical** | Every sortie carries: authorization class (COA / 107 waiver / SGI), RPIC party, VO/DAA mitigation, authorization window, one-tap abort to RTH. Standing orders may not substitute for the RPIC. “Drones are autonomous — the pairing is a standing order, not a pilot” (`commandConsole.ts:1717`) is the most dangerous legal sentence in the product. |
| Multiple aircraft, one console | FT-01, FT-02, FT-03 from one seat. Battery handover is a card. | 14 CFR § 107.35: operating multiple small UAS with one remote pilot requires a waiver. | Same as above; the waiver file will be Exhibit 1. | **Critical** | Fleet size is a licensed envelope, not a UI count. Console must refuse to arm sortie N+1 outside the waiver. |
| Night operations, 03:47 PST | Night epoch is a timestamp and a dark theme. | § 107.29: night flight allowed **with anti-collision lighting** (post-2021); waiver required without it. Public-safety SGI/wildfire waivers exist (FAA SOSC) but are documents, not themes. | Operator / RPIC. | **High** | Night is an authorization condition, not a color scheme. Anti-collision light state, waiver id, and SOSC contact belong on the fleet widget. |
| Flight over people / evacuation guidance | FT-03 paired to Vance for “Speaker + strobes route guide.” `stall` option: “Send FT-03 · speaker + strobes.” | § 107.39: flight over human beings requires Category 1–4 compliance or a waiver. Broadcasting evacuation instructions from a UAS over a fleeing public is the most legally exposed payload in the product. | RPIC, agency, vendor; a struck-person incident is felony-adjacent. | **Critical** | Speaker/strobes is a **separate authorization**, never a standing order, never a recommended-first option without the Category/waiver chip on the same row. |
| Airspace deconfliction with crewed tankers | Cole’s radio: “keep your drones under one-five-zero, tankers on the drop.” TFR label `UAS FLOOR < 150′`. | A low-flying lead plane or helicopter inside a 91.137 TFR has the right-of-way. A UAS under 150′ AGL in the same box is still inside the TFR. “Under 150” is not deconfliction. No ADS-B in, no DAA, no lost-C2 legal procedure beyond SO-3 failsafe return at 8% battery. | Federal exposure if a UAS enters tanker airspace; NTSB “probable cause” will name the UAS program and the console that made the incursion look like a map style. | **Critical** | Airspace is a **received, staleable, hard** layer. Console cannot authorize a plan whose geometry enters an active TFR above the UAS floor. Lost C2 is a legal event (notify Air Attack + FAA), not a battery widget. |
| Remote ID / identification | Absent. | 14 CFR Part 89 Remote ID is in force for the fleet the product imagines. | FAA enforcement; the record cannot answer “whose aircraft was that.” | **High** | Remote ID status per airframe; missing Remote ID = no launch. |
| Public vs civil aircraft | Not modeled. A county OES / CAL FIRE dock program is likely a **public aircraft operation** (49 U.S.C. § 40102) — COA path, not Part 107. | Using Part 107 language for a public aircraft, or vice versa, is a program-level legal error. | Agency counsel. Vendor must support both envelopes. | **High** | Program type is a setup fact. The authority popover should print it. |

## 4 · Public alerting & civil liability

| Domain | What the product does | Real-world requirement / failure state | Liability vector | Sev. | Defensible design |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Who may send a WEA | Plan gate prices “WEA ≈ 917 devices · reverse-911 ≈ 172 lines” and splits `reqs: WEA · reverse-911 → Sheriff / County OES`. Lin’s bridge role is “EOC alert officer · statutory issuer,” net “IPAWS / WEA.” Ack is scripted radio: “OES copies. WEA accepted — 917 devices.” | WEAs are originated by **authorized Alerting Authorities** (state, local, tribal, territorial public safety officials, NWS, NCMEC, the President) through FEMA IPAWS. A commercial or AI software system **cannot** originate a WEA (Ready.gov; FEMA). The product’s REQ split is directionally correct and then undermines itself by putting “statutory issuer” on a contact-sheet string and by letting a console click be described as “Evacuation order issued on your authorization.” | Unauthorized origination (federal) if the vendor ever wires this to IPAWS. If the agency uses the console as the origination path without an authorized official: the official, the agency, and the vendor. California public-entity liability is no-liability-except-by-statute (Gov. Code § 815) plus discretionary-act immunity (Gov. Code § 820.2) — **but** discretionary immunity does not cover a defective **operational** act, and an undertaken warning that increases harm or fails to warn a known in-path population is the fact pattern that defeats immunity. | **Critical** | Console may **draft** a WEA and **requisition** it. It may never send. Issued banner names the originating authorized official as a party, the IPAWS message id as received state, and cap/cancel/expire as received state. Geofence “no spill beyond 150 m” is a **check**, not a legal opinion. |
| False alarm vs delayed warning | Lapse = nothing is sent + escalate to supervisor. Right default. No false-alarm cost on the card (only reach). No “warn now, order later” split as a first-class legal act. | The two failure modes that get agencies sued are (a) alerting the wrong people (cry-wolf, non-evacuation next time) and (b) alerting too late (deaths in the path). Product prices reach and geofence, and prices silence, but not **earliness vs certainty** as a legal trade. | Agency (cry-wolf deaths on the next fire; delayed-warning deaths on this one). Vendor if the UI made “wait for FT-01” the recommended first option where the law wanted an immediate warning. | **High** | Every public-warning card carries three clocks: time-to-confirm, time-to-order, time-to-alert, and the residual miss distance. Recommended-first is allowed; the legal residual is printed, not inferred. |
| Multilingual / accessibility of the alert | English-only card copy. No Spanish, no ASL, no JIC/rumor control, no 911-surge model. | California WEA practice is bilingual (Spanish at minimum) in most OES jurisdictions; IPAWS supports it. English-only WEAs are a civil-rights / effective-warning problem. Local emergency plans must address AFN populations. | Agency; ADA / civil-rights exposure for access-and-functional-needs populations. | **High** | Drafted WEA includes language set and AFN channels as requisition fields. |
| The public as a party | 917 devices, 37 + 345 households, “acknowledgements coming in” as a radio quote. No resident ack, no 911 surge, no inverse-911 line status as a legal act, no EAS. | The public is not a stakeholder in this console. They are a reach number. That is the design error the brief’s “fleeing public” asked the product not to make. | Wrongful death (failed warning); the vendor’s “90 seconds to 47 households” marketing will be read to the jury. | **High** | Warning is a two-state legal object (requisitioned / originated). Lin’s radio “acknowledgements” are **not** public acknowledgements. |

## 6 · Ranked Top-10 legal failure states

1. **Unnamed RPIC / unlicensed BVLOS fleet inside a wildfire TFR** — federal aviation violation; mid-air catastrophic exposure. *(Critical)*
2. **Single non-statutory “operator” authorizing an evacuation/WEA** — the console designs a person the law does not recognize. *(Critical)*
3. **WEA as a UI requisition with no IPAWS origination chain** — unauthorized or fictional public warning. *(Critical)*
4. **`message_unit` unguarded transmit** — model words become world-effects the truth guard never sees. *(Critical)*
5. **Export is not a record** — no custodian, no comms log, no hash chain; the product’s own “not legal attestation” copy will be used against any claim that the logs are defensible. *(Critical)*
6. **Dissent-as-exhibit** — every tactical alternate written as a safety disagreement. *(Critical)*
7. **Speaker/strobes over a fleeing public without Category 1–4 / waiver** — § 107.39. *(Critical)*
8. **“Mandatory evacuation” vocabulary + hardcoded A. Rao + supervisor-as-string** — the record cannot name who legally acted. *(High)*
9. **Live-model path never smoke-tested; key in localStorage** — the honesty contract is unverified on the only path that will be used in an EOC. *(High)*
10. **Exercise disclaimer vs live dual use** — the product is one installation away from being used for the thing it says it is not. *(High)*

## 7 · What the product got legally right (do not undo)

- Two-tier authority with citations; uncited standing dropped.
- Silence is never approval; gated lapse sends nothing and escalates.
- OWNED vs REQ on the gate face is a real sketch of delegated authority.
- Ack loop + ORDER ISSUED · ACK PENDING is the right requisition skeleton (still scripted).
- Contain arc: authorities always notified, even with no evac — the most legally mature beat in the product.
- Dissent + new-evidence re-raise past the operator is a real governance mechanism. The trigger is what has to move, not the record.
- The export copy refuses legal inflation. Keep that copy.
