The other end of the radio — ATAK companion, two frames

Design artifact, not a product. The brief's ground teams exist; the console's TAK push today is one-way. These two frames close the loop on an 8-inch ruggedized tablet — the field surface, deliberately not a phone.

RULINGS THIS ARTIFACT CARRIES: phones stay out of scope — the field surface is the 8" ruggedized tablet crews already mount. What the field receives is SENT (assignment, route, hazard pins, geofence). What the field can SEND BACK is bounded: route-blocked / objective-reached / position — three marks, each landing in the console as MEA with the unit's name on it. The field never gates, never authorizes, never edits the plan. Free text rides the radio, where it already lives.

  • The assignment, in the field's own terms: which route to keep open, the condition it stays open under, and the geofence — the same fragility line the operator's gate carried, translated to a task.

Frame 2 · how the field talks back

  • Three marks, not a keyboard. Route blocked / objective reached / position — the only field inputs. Bounded input is why the return path can be trusted as MEA: a mark is an observation with a name on it, not a message to interpret.
  • The mark lands with its consequence. The receipt back to the crew shows what their mark caused in the console — route cut, alternate drafted, WEA update requisitioned. The field sees that reporting matters, which is what keeps reporting honest.
  • Free text stays on the radio. The field IC's words enter the record as quotes through dispatch — as they already do in the scenario — not as a second chat surface.
